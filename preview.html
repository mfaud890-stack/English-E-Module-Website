Learning Objectives

After completing this module, students are expected to:

  • Identify expressions for asking opinions, giving opinions, agreeing, and disagreeing in short dialogues.
  • Use polite expressions such as What do you think about...?, In my opinion..., I agree because..., and I disagree because....
  • Respond to friends' opinions using simple reasons related to school life, social media, games, and learning technology.
  • Create and perform a short opinion dialogue with a partner using appropriate vocabulary, pronunciation, and interaction.

Main Material: Asking and Giving Opinions

An opinion is a personal idea, belief, or feeling about something. In English, we often ask and give opinions in discussions, group work, classroom conversations, and online interactions.

Asking for Opinions

ExpressionUse
What do you think about this idea?General question
How do you feel about using AI for homework?Personal feeling
Do you think games can help us learn?Yes/no opinion
What is your opinion on school uniforms?Formal opinion

Giving Opinions

ExpressionUse
I think it is helpful.Simple opinion
In my opinion, it makes learning easier.Clear personal view
From my point of view, students need balance.More formal response
I believe it can improve our confidence.Stronger belief

Agreeing

I agree. / That's true. / Exactly. / I have the same opinion.

Disagreeing Politely

I see your point, but... / I understand, but I think... / I disagree because...

Language Focus: A good opinion is stronger when it has a reason. Use this pattern: Opinion + because + reason. Example: I think phones are useful because they help students find vocabulary quickly.

Material Summary

This section summarizes the main points students have learned in this e-module.

1. Purpose of the material: Students learn how to ask for opinions, give personal opinions, and respond to other people’s ideas in polite English.
2. Asking for opinions: To invite someone’s idea, students can use expressions such as What do you think about...?, How do you feel about...?, and What is your opinion on...?
3. Giving opinions: To state an idea clearly, students can use expressions such as I think..., In my opinion..., I believe..., and From my point of view...
4. Giving reasons: A strong opinion should include a reason. Students can use because to connect an opinion with an explanation, for example: I think short videos are useful because they make vocabulary easier to remember.
5. Agreeing and disagreeing: Students can agree by saying I agree with you or That’s a good point. They can disagree politely by saying I see your point, but... or I respect your opinion, however...
6. Communication focus: The main skill in this lesson is not only speaking English correctly, but also communicating respectfully, listening to others, and explaining ideas with confidence.
